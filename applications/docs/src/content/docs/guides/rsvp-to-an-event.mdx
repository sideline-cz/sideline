Every event you're invited to gets an RSVP prompt — in Discord, on the web, or both. Responding is a single click for **Yes** or **No**, and two steps — click, then add a required note — for **Coming later** or **Not sure**.

## What the responses mean

- **Yes** — I'll be there, on time. Counts as present for attendance stats.
- **Coming later** — I'll be there, but arriving late. Counts as present for attendance stats, roster headcounts, and team generation, exactly like **Yes** — the only difference is you're letting your team know to expect you after the start time. Choosing this response requires a short note explaining when you expect to arrive (e.g. "~7pm after work") — you can't submit it blank.
- **Not sure** — you don't know yet whether you'll make it. Unlike **Coming later**, this does **not** count as attending: you're not included in confirmed headcounts, you don't get a roster slot (or its Discord role) on events linked to a roster, you're not put into generated teams, and training attendance isn't auto-logged for you. It still requires a short note — say what your answer depends on (e.g. "depends on work") — you can't submit it blank either.
- **No** — I won't be there. Counts as an explicit decline, not a no-show.

The key difference between **No** and silence: **No** tells your captain you got the message. Silence doesn't. That's why reminders keep nudging people who haven't answered.

<Aside type="note" title="Reminders stop after repeated non-responses">
  If you have a **Player** role and miss a configurable number of events in a row without responding, the bot stops sending you RSVP reminder DMs. Respond to any event — yes, coming later, not sure, or no — to reset your streak and start receiving reminders again. Your captain can adjust the threshold in **Team settings → Discord → Stop reminding after N missed RSVPs**.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Switching away from Coming later or Not sure clears your note">
  Your **Coming later** or **Not sure** note only makes sense for the response it was written for — one answers "when will you arrive?", the other "what does it depend on?" So switching to a different response clears it. Switching straight between **Coming later** and **Not sure** without typing a new note is rejected — you'll need to add one first. If the event is linked to a roster with approval required, switching from **Coming later** straight to **Not sure** also withdraws you from the roster; answering **Coming later** again puts you back in the pending queue rather than straight back onto the roster.
</Aside>

## Responding in Discord

<Steps>

1. Find the event post in your private personal events channel (or via `/event list`). It shows the date, time, location, and current counts.
2. Click **Yes**, **Coming later**, **Not sure**, or **No**. The buttons are under the event details.
3. **Yes** and **No** save immediately — the bot confirms your response right away, and the post itself refreshes with your name in the right bucket within a few seconds. **Coming later** and **Not sure** instead open a pop-up asking for a required note (your ETA for one, what it depends on for the other); fill it in and submit to record your response.

</Steps>

<Aside type="note" title="&quot;We don't know you yet&quot;?">
  Some teams require a complete profile before you can RSVP. If you see this message instead of a confirmation, tap the **Finish my profile** button, fill in your name, date of birth, and gender, then tap your RSVP button again — it isn't retried for you automatically.
</Aside>

## Responding on the web

<Steps>

1. Open the Sideline web app and go to your team's Events tab.
2. Click an event to see details.
3. Click the RSVP button you want. **Yes** and **No** update the count live. Clicking **Coming later** or **Not sure** reveals a required note field below the buttons — type your note and click **Save note**; the button stays disabled until the note is filled in.

</Steps>

## Changing your mind

You can change your RSVP at any time until the event starts — or until your team's RSVP deadline, if one is set. Click a different button; the previous answer is overwritten. Captains see the change immediately.

### RSVP deadlines

By default RSVPs stay open right up to the moment an event starts. A team can close them earlier so the captain gets a settled headcount, from **Team settings → General**:

| Setting | What it controls |
|---|---|
| **Close RSVPs before start (hours)** | How many hours before the start RSVPs stop accepting changes. Leave it blank to keep them open until the event starts. `0` closes them exactly at the start. |
| **Per event type** | Optional per-event-type deadlines that override the team-wide value. Each row is **Inherit** (use the value above), **Hours** (a number of its own), or **Off** (no early deadline for that type). |

Once the deadline passes you can still see the event and the answer you gave — you just can't change it. The event page and the Discord card both show the deadline before it arrives, so it should never be a surprise.

<Aside type="caution" title="All-day events">
  For an all-day event the deadline counts back from **midnight at the start of the first day**, not from the time people actually turn up. A weekend tournament with a team-wide 24-hour deadline therefore closes on Friday morning. If that is too early, set that event type to **Off** in the **Per event type** grid, or give it a smaller number of its own.

  Note that `0` is not the same as **Off** here. With no deadline set, an all-day event stays open for answers right through its last day. Setting `0` closes it at midnight on the first day — earlier than the default, not later. **Off** is what keeps the original behaviour.

  One edge case: if your team changes its timezone *after* an all-day event was created, that event's deadline is still measured from the midnight that applied when it was created. Recreate the event if you need it re-anchored.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="After the event">
  Once the event has ended, RSVPs are locked for attendance records. If you need to mark yourself as present after the fact (e.g. you forgot to click), ask your captain to adjust it from the event page.
</Aside>

## What if I'm running late?

Use the **Coming later** response instead of **Yes** — add a short note with your expected arrival time so the rest of the team (and your captain) knows to expect you after the start. It still counts as attending, so you won't show up as a no-show or get left out of roster/team-generation headcounts.

## What if I don't know yet?

Use **Not sure** rather than leaving the event unanswered — it resets your reminder streak the same as any other response, and your captain can see at a glance that you've seen the invite, just haven't decided (add a short note saying what it depends on). Keep in mind it doesn't count toward the confirmed headcount, so switch to **Yes** or **Coming later** as soon as you know you're in.
